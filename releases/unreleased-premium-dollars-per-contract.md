# Unreleased — trade Premium is dollars per contract

**Behaviour change to trade-log imports and analytics.** `Premium` is dollars for one contract (one lot), regardless of whether its CSV cell contains a decimal point: `250` and `250.00` both mean $250 per lot. Position premium is the absolute per-contract value multiplied by the contract count. Option Omega's EMA row with $420 premium and three contracts has $1,260 total premium and $1,251 net P/L, or 99.2857% premium efficiency, independently of margin. A padded `.00` OO premium no longer inflates efficiency through a guessed option multiplier. MFE/MAE dollar excursions use the same total-premium basis.

**Existing CSV authors:** a per-share quote such as `2.50` in the old examples must be written as `250` for a $250-per-lot premium. Leaving `2.50` makes premium efficiency and MFE/MAE dollar amounts 100× smaller. Trade-log `Premium` is distinct from reporting-log `Initial Premium`; the Trading Calendar shows backtest premiums as an explicitly labeled $/share quote to compare with OO reporting quotes, without changing the stored dollar premium. Programmatic callers must remove `Trade.premiumPrecision`; the field is no longer accepted by the model or validator.

Existing browser blocks retain their stored numeric premiums without re-import or migration; any old `premiumPrecision` property is ignored when calculating. IndexedDB combined-trades and performance-snapshot cache keys change so old derived entries are not served after upgrade. The old cache entries remain inert until the block's cache is cleared. This note does not bump a version or publish a release.
